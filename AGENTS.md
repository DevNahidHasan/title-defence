# Workspace Rules: Brainstorming & Title Defense

## Core Directive
This workspace is primarily dedicated to **brainstorming, idea generation, and solution design** (specifically for academic/project title defense, research topics, problem formulations, and methodology design).

Unless explicitly instructed to perform other tasks (such as detailed code implementation, deep mathematical proofs, or paper writing), all agent responses must adhere to the following principles:

---

### 1. Primary Focus
* **Ideation & Innovation:** Propose fresh, defensible, and relevant project/thesis concepts, problem statements, and high-level architectural solutions.
* **Academic & Practical Feasibility:** Evaluate ideas based on novelty, feasibility, research gaps, methodology soundness, and defense readiness.
* **Structured Brainstorming:** Present ideas with clear problem statements, proposed solutions, expected outcomes, and potential trade-offs.

---

### 2. Avoid Extra Calculations & Over-Engineering
* **No Unnecessary Math / Calculations:** Avoid unnecessary low-level mathematical derivations, tedious benchmark calculations, or granular computation steps unless the user explicitly requests them.
* **Keep High-Level & Actionable:** Focus on concept clarity, intuition, and architectural reasoning rather than excessive theoretical or numerical detours.

---

### 3. Response Style & Workflow
* **Concise & Direct:** Provide well-structured comparisons, bullet points, and actionable alternatives to facilitate quick decision-making.
* **Interactive Exploration:** Help narrow down, refine, or combine ideas iteratively.
* **Adherence to Scope:** Maintain this ideation/solutioning posture until the user explicitly directs you to perform a different mode of work.

---

### 4. Blueprint & Artifact Modification Gate
* **Explicit Approval Required:** Never alter, overwrite, or update any project blueprint, specification document, or artifact without the user's explicit permission. Always discuss proposed changes in chat first and wait for clear confirmation before modifying the blueprint.

---

### 5. Rule Synchronization Maintenance
* **Dual File Maintenance:** Any rule modifications, additions, or removals must always be mirrored identically across both `GEMINI.md` and `AGENTS.md`.

---

### 6. Tone & Vocabulary for Academic / Teacher Submissions
* **Undergraduate Student Voice:** Whenever drafting or revising documents, proposal forms, slides, or reports intended for course teachers, supervisors, or defense panels, write in simple, clear, student-friendly English. Avoid artificial, overly dense academic jargon, high-sounding corporate buzzwords, and obvious AI-generated cliches. The writing must sound authentic, natural, and like it was genuinely written by an undergraduate student.

---

### 7. Consistent Entity and Role Referencing
* **Standard Role Terminology:** Always refer to project users and stakeholders with clear, consistent terms across all documentation, proposals, and presentations:
  - **Vehicle Owner:** A person (driving a car or motorcycle) searching for and booking parking. When contextually appropriate, can also be referred to as **vehicle driver** or **car/bike owner**.
  - **Parking Spot Owner:** The primary term for someone renting out an unused garage space. Depending on the sentence context (e.g., discussing building ownership vs. slot rental), natural contextual references like **residential flat owner**, **flat owner**, or **garage space owner** may be used to keep sentence flow natural.
  - **Security Guard:** The residential building gatekeeper who verifies incoming vehicles at the gate.
  - **Admin:** The platform administrator who verifies Parking Spot Owner documents (KYC) and oversees the system.
* **Consistent Platform Reference:** Refer to the project consistently as **"Nirapod Parking"** or **"the platform / the system"**. Avoid erratic switching between pronouns (such as mixing "we", "our team", "the project", and "the company").
